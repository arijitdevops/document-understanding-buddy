# Home Solar Buyer's Guide

## 1. How Solar Panels Work

Solar panels are made of photovoltaic (PV) cells, usually built from silicon. When sunlight
hits a cell, it knocks electrons loose and creates a direct current (DC). An **inverter**
converts that DC into the alternating current (AC) used by household appliances.

A typical residential panel today is rated between 400 and 450 watts. The rating describes
output under standard test conditions (1,000 W/m² of sunlight at 25 °C); real-world output is
usually 10-25% lower because of heat, shading, dust and the angle of the sun.

## 2. Sizing a System

To estimate the system size you need, divide your annual electricity use by the expected
production per kilowatt of panels in your region.

- Annual use: check twelve months of utility bills (for example 9,000 kWh).
- Production factor: 1,200-1,600 kWh per installed kW per year in most of the United States.
- Example: 9,000 kWh ÷ 1,400 kWh/kW ≈ 6.4 kW, or about 15 panels of 430 W.

Roof orientation matters: south-facing roofs (in the northern hemisphere) produce the most
energy, while east- and west-facing roofs typically produce 10-20% less.

## 3. Costs and Incentives

Installed prices commonly fall between $2.50 and $3.50 per watt before incentives, so a 6.4 kW
system costs roughly $16,000-$22,000. Many countries offer tax credits or rebates; check the
current rules where you live, because they change frequently.

**Net metering** lets you send surplus electricity to the grid in exchange for bill credits.
Under net billing, exports are credited at a lower rate than the price you pay for imports,
which makes self-consumption and batteries more valuable.

## 4. Batteries

A home battery stores surplus solar energy for use in the evening or during outages. Common
lithium iron phosphate (LFP) batteries hold 10-15 kWh and are rated for 6,000 or more charge
cycles. A battery is most worthwhile when export credits are low, electricity prices vary by
time of day, or backup power is important.

## 5. Maintenance and Lifespan

- Clean panels once or twice a year, or after dust storms and heavy pollen seasons.
- Inspect wiring and mounting hardware after severe weather.
- Monitor production in the inverter app; a sudden drop usually points to shading, dirt or a
  failed component.

Panels typically carry a 25-year performance warranty guaranteeing at least 80-87% of the
original output. Inverters usually need replacing once during the life of the system, after
10-15 years.

## 6. Glossary of Common Terms

- **kW (kilowatt):** a unit of power, the rate at which energy is produced.
- **kWh (kilowatt-hour):** a unit of energy; running 1 kW for one hour.
- **Inverter:** converts DC from the panels into AC for the home.
- **Net metering:** crediting exported electricity at the retail rate.
- **Degradation:** the slow loss of panel output over time, about 0.5% per year.
